# EP004 — Pompeii's Final Day
**Status:** Preproduction research and narrative architecture v1, not a final fact-checked script or footage order. No generation costs.

## Central premise
Walk through the final day(s) of Pompeii during the AD 79 eruption of Vesuvius, anchored in archaeology rather than a fictional omniscient eyewitness. Avoid presenting any single family's invented journey as established fact.

## Evidence and uncertainty checklist for independent fact-check
1. **Date disputed:** Pliny the Younger manuscripts traditionally support 24 August, but autumnal archaeological clues and an October charcoal inscription complicate the calendar. Avoid an unqualified exact date; explain debate.
2. **Pliny's testimony:** His letters to Tacitus were written decades after the event and describe the eruption and his uncle's death from Misenum/Stabiae perspectives; he was not an eyewitness inside Pompeii.
3. **Eruption sequence:** initial towering eruption column, pumice and ash fallout, later deadly pyroclastic density currents. Exact timing and local impacts require source-specific caveats.
4. **Pompeii vs Herculaneum:** separate locations and different burial/death mechanisms; do not interchange visuals or describe every Pompeii victim as killed instantly by lava.
5. **Ash and pumice:** roofs could fail under accumulated load; streets and escape routes became dangerous; portray ash darkness without assuming uniform conditions everywhere.
6. **Deaths:** avoid definitive population or death toll claims; excavation records cannot directly establish all people present or killed.
7. **Everyday life:** forum, bakeries, fountains, household shrines, street commerce, baths and social inequality are supported categories; avoid anachronistic clothing, architecture and tools.
8. **Scientific limits:** distinguish reconstructed eruption phases from artistic dramatization and note that details of individual final moments are often unknowable.

## Proposed nine chapters, 11–13 minute target
1. A City That Doesn't Know (ordinary life and ominous mountain)
2. Warning Signs (earthquakes, ambiguous precursors)
3. The Sky Breaks (eruption column)
4. The Rain of Stone (pumice and roofs)
5. The Streets Go Dark (movement, confusion, choices)
6. The Hours Stretch (ash burden and danger)
7. A Deadly Change (pyroclastic currents, with caveats)
8. Buried and Forgotten? (aftermath and preservation, avoiding myth of complete forgetting)
9. What the Ash Revealed (archaeology, empathy, uncertainty)

## Editorial visual direction
Photorealistic 16:9, moving shots only, each unique; use wide city geography, interior daily-life details, mountain plume physics, street-level ash deposition, and later excavation. No repetitive aerial plumes to fill time; no spectacle-only montage of graphic suffering. Strong dynamic shot variety while respecting historical plausibility. Separate Herculaneum if depicted, with location cues. Original atmospheric score and Fraser narration when supported.

## Sources to check before final script lock

## Original narration
Why does this black liquid suddenly grow spikes? It's called ferrofluid: tiny magnetic particles suspended in a carrier liquid. Bring a magnetic field close, and the particles respond together. Magnetic forces pull the surface upward, while gravity and surface tension resist. When the magnetic force becomes strong enough, a flat surface breaks into an orderly forest of peaks. Scientists call it the Rosensweig instability. Remove the field, and the spikes can collapse back into liquid. It isn't alive. It's physics making a pattern. Scenario Files. Beyond the Known.

## Scientific guardrails
Ferrofluid is colloid of nanoscale magnetic particles in a carrier liquid, typically stabilized by surfactant. Spikes form above critical magnetic field in Rosensweig/normal-field instability: magnetic forces versus gravity and surface tension. No magnets mysteriously alive, no permanent solid needles.

## Final short narration (Fraser)
What happens if you fire a bullet into a hurricane? Does the wind stop it? Throw it backwards? No. A bullet travels much faster than hurricane winds, so it keeps moving forward. But it won't fly through untouched. Powerful crosswinds can shift its path sideways. Heavy rain and turbulence add drag and uncertainty. Meanwhile, the storm is so enormous, it doesn't even notice. One piece of metal against a system hundreds of kilometres across. Scenario Files. Beyond the Known.

## Factual and safety notes
- Hurricanes are giant rotating storms with sustained winds of 119 km/h or greater (NOAA NHC), and Category 5 winds reach 252 km/h or greater; physical projectile speed typically greatly exceeds wind speed. No claim the hurricane blows a bullet backwards or stops it outright.
- Crosswinds can alter flight direction; drag, turbulence and heavy rainfall complicate the scenario. Exact drift depends on numerous conditions, so no precise trajectory asserted.
- There is no actual hurricane firearm operation footage, procedural shooting guidance or human targets; all images are fictional AI-generated physics visualisations. Never attempt firing weapons in hazardous weather.
- Source: https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/aboutsshws.php (NOAA National Hurricane Center Saffir-Simpson scale).

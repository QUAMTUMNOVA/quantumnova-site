## Original narration
What happens when you drop a strong magnet through a copper pipe? It seems to fall through invisible syrup. Copper isn't attracted to magnets like iron. But the moving magnet creates swirling electric currents inside the pipe. Those currents generate magnetic fields that push back against its motion. That's Lenz's law. The magnet keeps falling, just much more slowly. Through a plastic tube, it drops normally. Nothing is sticking to the copper. Its lost energy becomes a tiny amount of heat. Scenario Files. ## Research & portrayal
Physics: changing magnetic flux through conductive nonferromagnetic copper induces eddy currents (Lenz's law), creating opposing magnetic field and slowing falling magnet. Copper doesn't attract like iron, but is conductive; effect stronger with strong magnets/thick pipes. Magnet continues to descend; motion energy becomes minute resistive heat. No giant sparks or levitation.

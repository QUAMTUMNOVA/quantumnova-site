## Narration
Why do tiny water droplets sometimes skate across a hot pan instead of vanishing instantly? At a high enough surface temperature, part of the water flashes into vapour. That vapour forms a thin cushion beneath the drop, keeping most of the liquid from touching the metal. With less direct contact, the droplet can glide around and evaporate more slowly than you'd expect. It's called the Leidenfrost effect. The drop isn't defying gravity. It's riding its own escaping steam. Scenario Files. Beyond the Known.

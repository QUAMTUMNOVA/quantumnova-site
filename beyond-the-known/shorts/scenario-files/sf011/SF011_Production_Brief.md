## Original narration
What happens when a soap bubble freezes? On an icy surface, in the right conditions, tiny ice crystals can spread through its incredibly thin liquid skin. Sometimes, crystals break loose and swirl around like a miniature snow globe. Scientists found that flowing liquid, driven by changing surface tension as ice forms, creates the effect. But it doesn't happen every time. When the surrounding air is warmer, a bubble may freeze only partly, then slowly collapse. It's not magic. It's heat, surface tension, and incredibly fragile ice.

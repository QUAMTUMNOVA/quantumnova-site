## Fraser narration
What happens when red-hot metal touches ice? Heat rushes from the metal into the frozen surface, melting a channel of water. Some of that water can flash into steam, hissing and bubbling around the contact point. When conditions are hot enough, a thin vapour layer may briefly separate the metal from the liquid, slowing heat transfer. The metal keeps cooling as the ice melts beneath it. This isn't ice exploding. It's heat, water, and changing phases, all fighting for the same space. Scenario Files. Beyond the Known.
